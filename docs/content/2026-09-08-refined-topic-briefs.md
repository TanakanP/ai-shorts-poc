## 1. Stop-sign silhouette — KEEP, develop first

**Topic:** A Stop Sign Can Speak Without Words
**Opening direction:** “This sign gives itself away before you read it.”
**Pattern:** FAMILIARITY_TWIST
**Audience:** U.S. everyday drivers.
**Angle:** Recognition through a familiar outline, rather than a lecture about sign design.
**Proof needed:** A clearly identifiable stop sign seen from behind, followed by its front.
**Search starts:** `back of stop sign`; `octagonal stop sign`; `stop sign close up`.
**Missing-shot rule:** Hold if the eight-sided outline is obscured. Generic traffic footage
cannot demonstrate recognition. Do not assume the renderer can remove lettering.

**Creative brief to use:**
Pattern: FAMILIARITY_TWIST. Begin with the back of a familiar roadside sign and an
unresolved recognition claim. Context removes the readable word; the reset reveals
that its outline still supplies a clue. Explain the distinctive eight sides, then
return to the same sign from the front. Keep this a visual recognition demonstration.
Do not claim universal recognition, invent the historical reason for the shape, or
suggest a sign facing another direction controls the viewer's lane. No acronyms.

**Fact packet:** PennDOT identifies STOP as the highway sign with an octagonal shape.
The recognition demonstration is our editorial treatment, not a measured perception
result. [PennDOT driver manual, Signs](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dmv/driver-services/pennsylvania-drivers-manual/online-drivers-manual/signs)

## 2. Rumble strips — KEEP, pending close-up footage

**Topic:** These Road Grooves Turn Your Tires Into an Alarm
**Opening direction:** “These road grooves make your tires sound an alarm.”
**Pattern:** BALANCED_REVEAL
**Audience:** Drivers who recognize the roadside rumble but have not seen its source.
**Angle:** A physical warning made by the road surface.
**Proof needed:** Visible milled grooves beside a lane, ideally with licensed footage
showing a tire crossing them. Do not substitute a speed bump or pothole.
**Search starts:** `road shoulder rumble strips`; `milled rumble strips close up`;
`highway edge grooves`.
**Missing-shot rule:** Hold if grooves cannot be seen clearly. Authentic sound would help,
but the current pipeline mutes stock audio; do not promise an audible demonstration.

**Creative brief to use:**
Pattern: BALANCED_REVEAL. Follow one set of milled shoulder grooves. Start with the
unexpected road-as-alarm claim. Establish their position beside the lane. Reset:
the warning is felt as well as heard. Explain that tires dropping into the grooves
create noise and vehicle vibration. Pay off with the road surface providing a warning
when a driver drifts. Avoid lists of strip types, crash statistics, guaranteed recovery,
and any invitation to drive onto them for a test.

**Fact packet:** FHWA describes milled grooves producing tire noise and vehicle vibration;
shoulder/edge strips warn drivers who drift from the lane. This brief concerns milled
strips specifically. [FHWA design and construction](https://highways.fhwa.dot.gov/safety/rwd/keep-vehicles-road/rumble-strips/design-construction)

## 3. Exit-number distance — REFRAME; HOLD for proof visuals

**Topic:** Exit Numbers Can Reveal the Miles Between Them
**Opening direction:** “These exit numbers hide a useful distance clue.”
**Pattern:** FAMILIARITY_TWIST
**Audience:** U.S. road-trip passengers and drivers.
**Angle:** A useful subtraction, replacing the misleading “how far you've driven” promise.
**Proof needed:** Two readable exit numbers on the same mileage-numbered route, with
verified context. An explicitly illustrative pair, 120 and 145, could demonstrate
roughly 25 miles; custom number graphics are not currently created by the pipeline.
**Search starts:** `highway exit number sign`; `interstate exit sign close up`;
`highway mile marker`.
**Missing-shot rule:** Generic signs cannot prove the specific calculation. Keep this on
hold until appropriate footage or an approved illustration workflow is available.

**Creative brief to use:**
Pattern: FAMILIARITY_TWIST. Contrast exit order with distance numbering. Build toward
one subtraction between two exits on the same mileage-numbered stretch. Reset:
a jump in numbers need not mean missing exits. Reveal the approximate distance and
close on the practical clue. Say “on mileage-numbered roads”; do not apply the rule
to every highway, cross a numbering reset, or equate one number with distance since
the viewer began driving. Keep the calculation visible, not merely spoken.

**Fact packet:** PennDOT explains that mileage-based exit numbering permits calculating
distance between exits. Our 120/145 pair is illustrative, not a verified named route.
[PennDOT driver manual, Exit Numbers](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dmv/driver-services/pennsylvania-drivers-manual/online-drivers-manual/signs)
